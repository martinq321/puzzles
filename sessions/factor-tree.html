What is a prime?

A whole number greater than 1 whose only positive factors are 1 and itself. A prime cannot split into two whole-number factors greater than 1.

For educators

The group view explains one selected multiplication. The prime fingerprint shows only prime leaves already discovered, with composite leaves still marked unresolved. No complete factorisation is supplied at the start.

Grouping identical prime numbers introduces powers. Pairing every prime factor identifies square numbers; grouping every prime factor into threes identifies cubes. Compare saved trees to investigate uniqueness of prime factorisation.

For large factors, grouping is schematic: counts are labelled and at most 20 groups are shown. Start with 30 or 48 for countable dots. Selecting a branch allows its split to be replaced; Undo restores it.
