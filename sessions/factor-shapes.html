How many shapes?

Use 30 tiles or cubes. Move the sliders and keep each different shape you find.

Choose every dimension yourself. Watch the total change, and try to reach the target exactly.

That is the whole task. There are no extra rules.

Two dimensions

Width × height counts the square tiles in a rectangle. The two numbers are a factor pair.

Three dimensions

Length × depth × height counts the little cubes in a cuboid. The three numbers are a factor triple.

Make one of your shapes with real tiles or cubes. Turn it around: is it a new shape, or the same one?

A small hint

Try keeping one dimension fixed while changing another. Which lengths can make exactly the target?

For educators

Learners choose all dimensions independently using integer-step sliders. No factors or missing dimensions are supplied. The running product allows trials below or above the target; only exact-target shapes enter the collection.

Target 30 has four rectangles and five cuboids, ignoring rotation. Target 48 has five rectangles and nine cuboids. Collections are separate for each target and dimension.

Continue to the cube investigations when learners are ready. A square is a special rectangle; a cube is a special cuboid. Integer targets 1–360 are supported.
