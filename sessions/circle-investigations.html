For educators · where to start

Start with investigation 1 and leave measurements hidden. Ask for one observation and one prediction before revealing numbers. There is no timer, scoring or requirement to finish the collection. The three prompts on each page offer increasing depth, rather than a test.

Investigations 1–3 explore angles; 4–5 introduce area and equal sides; 6–7 extend to circle centres and squares on triangle sides. Investigation 8 explores polygons and stars. Investigation 9 separates a shape from its appearance when tilted. No trigonometry is needed.

All circles with a fixed centre have radius 1, so lengths and areas use that unit. Rounded measurements are clues, not proofs. Degenerate triangles do not have three defined angles. In the free three-point investigation, the circle changes size to pass through the points; it is not fixed to radius 1.

Try these with paper circles, a ruler, string or a hinged cardboard triangle alongside the screen. Ask “Can you find a counterexample?” and “What could explain this?”
