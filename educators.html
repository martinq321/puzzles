MATHS WEDNESDAYS · EDUCATOR GUIDE

Easy to enter.
Room to think deeply.

An activity’s accessible starting point and its full mathematical analysis are different things. Choose a worthwhile destination for this learner, today.

1 · Entry

Play, manipulate, count and notice. Use physical objects where helpful.

2 · Investigation

Predict, organise examples, compare and test ideas.

3 · Explanation

Connect words, objects, diagrams and symbols. Explain why.

4 · Full analysis

Generalise, prove, analyse limitations and connect to further mathematics.

A map, not a ladder everyone must climb

Learners can move between these stages. Success at the entry stage is worthwhile in itself. Advanced analysis is available, not required for enjoying or completing an activity.

Age ranges below are provisional design estimates, not assessed ratings, admission requirements or labels of ability. Reading, working memory, motor access, confidence and previous experience can change the support needed. Distinguish a numerical challenge from the ability to operate the interface.

Screen, hands and consolidation

Use a digital representation to make changes visible, and inexpensive objects to let learners test the same idea physically. Sugar cubes, reusable cube blocks, paper squares, counters, cards, spaghetti and string can all be useful. Choose materials suited to the learner and setting.

After exploration, connect representations and consolidate the idea with a few purposeful examples, retrieval and a new context. Tutoring may prioritise investigation and offer consolidation as supported homework; it should remain flexible when practice is needed in the session.

A practical session sequence
  1. Offer an inviting question and a manageable first action.
  2. Invite a prediction before demonstrating.
  3. Pause to inspect and explain what changed.
  4. Compare with physical materials or a second representation.
  5. Record one mathematical insight.
  6. Revisit it later and test it in a different situation.
Using the random garden

At primary level, toss coins to decide seed types and compare runs. Later, investigate outcome tables, expectations and distributions. The first two days have fixed coin totals under the stated rules; offspring types first affect Day 3. A complete analysis of the branching process is much more advanced than participating in the experiment.

Support without removing the thinking

Reduce reading or memory demands, use labelled day cards and let learners choose a representation. Give a small hint or a direct explanation when needed. Avoid revealing an entire solution when the purpose is to investigate.

Something learners want to return to

Use screens when they make an idea clearer or enable an experiment. Mix them with physical materials, conversation and paper. Avoid turning an investigation into an endless compulsory exercise list.

Ask learners what they enjoy, dislike and would choose again. Frustration with a particular homework system is a reason to examine the task and context, not to label the learner. Enjoyment and mathematical learning both matter; neither proves the other.

A developing educational model

These pages are designed for investigation and teaching. They are not a validated assessment, a complete curriculum, or an established clinical intervention. Usability, engagement, mathematical understanding and transfer require different evidence.

Development should combine mathematical review, learner usability testing, independent educator trials and appropriately designed evaluations. A strong educator can deepen an activity; the instructions and interface should also support a reliable minimum experience without expert rescue.

Relevant reference frameworks: NCETM’s Five Big Ideas, EEF professional development guidance and EEF’s evaluation pipeline. These links indicate reference material, not endorsement.

Assessment should follow the mathematics

Record what learners demonstrate, the settings and support used, and whether understanding transfers to another task. Distinguish independent from scaffolded performance. Speed, clicks and abandoned games alone cannot establish a learner’s ability or a diagnosis.

Planned reports will summarise evidence, uncertainties and next steps for educator review. Suggested adaptations should respond to observed barriers and be trialled and reviewed. Automated reports and secure learner tracking are proposals, not features currently available on these pages.

From experimenting to writing proofs

Planned activities include finding counterexamples, repairing gaps, arranging statements with reasons, exploring invariants, writing induction steps and turning proof maps into prose. Supplied proof tiles are scaffolds: the eventual goal is an independent argument on a new problem.

Feedback should distinguish a convincing example from a general proof and permit more than one valid approach.
