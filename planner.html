Projects & development roadmap

Updated 25 October 2026 · Plans and requirements are separate from completed builds.

Teaching approach

Short, interesting hands-on investigations with optional screen support; learner choice, manageable challenge and one idea explored deeply. Professional, age-respectful presentation. Use physical companions: sugar cubes, card games, Hanoi, scales, paper and construction materials.

Website and navigation

Two separate sites: Maths Wednesdays for introduced student activities; puzzles for live testing and planning. One homepage card per main activity, with helpers inside. Sprouts and Robot Trails belong on the student menu; river crossing stays off until introduced. Keep planning and private records off the student site.

Shared interface requirements

Use the homepage font, clear compact controls and accessible undo arrows. Diagrams fit their available window; scale stays stable during motion, rotation and sorting. Show constraints visually. Educator notes sit behind a small book icon; an icon is not a login or privacy boundary.

Accounts, access and online play — planned

Authenticated teacher/student access with chosen activities per student; separate local profiles from real accounts. Online Mastermind needs shared game state, rooms, turn handling and a backend. Compare SQL and lower-cost backend options before selecting one. Future app and monetisation remain separate plans; no current cost or provider commitment is recorded.

Creative and relational work — planned

Optional world-building, village resources and fictional creator businesses. Explore links with creative/art-based practice, attachment and adverse experiences in educator guidance, while keeping maths tutoring distinct from therapy. Do not infer attachment styles or trauma histories from game behaviour.

Curriculum roadmap — planned

Original primary partitioning and division models; GCSE circle theorems, trig and algebra; A-level calculus, statistics and mechanics; junior/higher Olympiad and Euclidean geometry. UK curriculum connections with enrichment beyond it; exam-board mapping and coverage checking are still to do.

Questions and explanations — planned

Each developed topic can offer investigation, practice, test and a hidden worked solution. Use gradual hints, pause/step/back, linked diagrams and notation, alternative methods and proof reasons. Prioritise partitioning, balance scales and Euclidean geometry next; this is a proposed build order, not completed lessons.

Sources and originality

Youcubed, Jo Boaler, NRICH, Numberphile, White Rose, mathematical-games discussions and Project Euler are sources of inspiration. Record source trails and create original wording, assets and activities. Video/article leads are not evidence of finished curriculum coverage.

Current delivery status

Downloadable local builds exist; this update does not publish to GitHub. Cut & Rearrange has five verified geometric constructions and cut-count variants. Euclid retains take-away only; Cube Challenges is withdrawn. Other named revisions should be checked in the delivered files before calling them finished. Local planner notes remain on the browser/device that created them.

How to interpret the catalogue

These are planning suggestions, not a claim that every lesson is finished. Ages are estimated supported entry points, with no upper age limit. Challenge describes the proposed investigation, not every possible version. All ratings are editorial starting points, not validated assessments.

Depth 1–5: scope for explanations, connections and extensions. Strategy/logic 1–5: centrality of decisions and deductions. Physical fit: 1 mainly screen, 2 useful companion, 3 central to the activity. Preparation/build effort 1–3: low, medium, high; build effort concerns a future polished interactive page, not session preparation.

GCSE and A-level views show suggested connections, not exam-board coverage. Younger learners may enjoy concrete versions of advanced topics. Open-problem activities must distinguish examples, bounded computation and proof.

“Done” means you have used the activity, not that it is mastered or its website is finished. Untick it to revisit. Related pages are existing starting points requiring teaching review; they may not yet implement the proposed lesson.

For a future subscription, the strongest offer is a tested teaching sequence, useful feedback and a practical companion. This planner has no accounts or payments. Local browser storage is sufficient here; cross-device accounts would need a backend and durable storage.

Source trails: Project Euler · Gardner index · Winning Ways · NRICH · Numberphile: 15 game. Coding prompts here are original suggestions, not copied Project Euler problems or published solutions. Book trails are leads for further research, not claims that every suggested activity was checked against those books.
