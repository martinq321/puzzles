Curves: the next sequence

  1. Existing Curve Lab: ramp races, plotting, ellipse pool and parabolic reflection.
  2. Built in this pack: straight-string parabolic envelopes; fixed-area hyperbolas; ruled saddles; twisting hyperboloid towers.
  3. Planned — rectangular pool: drag the cue, predict a bounce, show the normal and equal incidence/reflection angles, unfold the table to turn a reflected path into a straight line.
  4. Planned — projectile motion: change launch speed and angle, pause at equal time intervals, compare horizontal and vertical motion. Use uniform gravity and no air resistance explicitly. Contrast a projectile's parabola with a brachistochrone's cycloid.
  5. Planned — conic sections: rotate a double cone and move a cutting plane; compare circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola. Include limiting/degenerate cases later.

Each should offer prediction, one-step animation, pause, touch manipulation and a cheap physical counterpart. Equations remain optional.

KS2 first; other stages later

Use England's National Curriculum as the objective map. Consult White Rose's small-step sequencing as a reference while writing original activities, explanations and artwork.

Proposed navigation: Key stage → Year/topic → Small learning goal → Explore → Practise → Explain → Revisit. Allow movement to prerequisite work without displaying a younger age label to the learner.

KS2 topic areas: number and place value; calculation; multiplication and division; fractions and decimals; measurement; geometry; statistics. Map exact content by year, with percentages, ratio and algebra where required.

Keep two labels in educator views: entry accessibility (language, arithmetic, motor and attention demands) and depth (reasoning, generalisation and proof). A rich investigation can have an easy entry point and advanced analysis.

For a new Year 5 learner: begin with enjoyable, untimed practical tasks. Observe quantity and place-value understanding, calculation strategies, mathematical vocabulary and response to different representations. Compare oral and written instructions; reduce steps and visual clutter; allow thinking time. Record what support changes performance. Do not infer a diagnosis from speed or one session.

With parental agreement, compare observations with the class teacher or SENCO. Agree specific learning goals and accommodations. Track starting strategy, support used, explanation, retention and independent transfer — not just correctness or time.

Suggested session pattern: familiar success → small investigation → brief practice → learner explains or demonstrates → optional home activity.
